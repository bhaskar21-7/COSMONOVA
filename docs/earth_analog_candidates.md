# Earth Analog Candidates

**Candidate Sites for Lunar and Martian Analog Comparison — COSMONOVA Candidate Dossier**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> No site listed here is assumed validated as an analog for our specific objectives. "Published analog use" means the literature uses the site as an analog for *some* purpose, which is not the same as validation for *our* use. Sites are presented as **candidates for investigation**, not as confirmed analogs.

## Detailed Candidate Profiles

### Atacama Desert (Chile)
- **Coordinates**: ~23–27°S, ~68–70°W
- **Elevation**: 2,000–5,000 m
- **Why considered**: Hyperaridity produces soils with very low organic content. Navarro-González et al. (2003, *Science*) demonstrated Mars-like soil chemistry.
- **Relevant properties**: Aridity, soil chemistry, UV exposure, volcanic substrate
- **Limitations**: Fog events occur at lower elevations; atmospheric pressure remains ~50–60 kPa at altitude (vs Mars ~0.6 kPa); gravity and radiation differ fundamentally.
- **Data availability**: Covered by SRTM, Copernicus DEM, Landsat, Sentinel-2. Good coverage.

### Devon Island / Haughton Crater (Canada)
- **Coordinates**: ~75.4°N, ~89.8°W
- **Crater diameter**: ~23 km
- **Why considered**: Haughton-Mars Project established it as a premier analog for impact geology and crewed field operations (Lee & Osinski 2005, *MAPS*).
- **Relevant properties**: Impact structure in sedimentary target, polar desert, periglacial terrain
- **Limitations**: Wetter than Mars; lichen patches indicate biological activity; at ~75°N, **SRTM does not cover this site** (coverage limit ~60°N). Must use ArcticDEM or Copernicus DEM.
- **Data availability**: ArcticDEM (~2 m); Landsat/Sentinel-2 seasonal; geological maps from GSC.

### Iceland — Volcanic Fields
- **Regions**: Askja caldera, Holuhraun lava field, Skaftáreldahraun, Mýrdalsjökull area
- **Why considered**: Basaltic lava fields with palagonite (weathered volcanic glass). Reportedly used in Apollo-era astronaut training [verify].
- **Relevant properties**: Basaltic substrate, lava morphologies, tuyas (subglacial volcanoes), hydrothermal alteration
- **Limitations**: Water-rich maritime climate; vegetation; dynamically active surface; Iceland is at ~63–66°N, near SRTM coverage limit.
- **Data availability**: ArcticDEM / Copernicus DEM; Sentinel-2; Icelandic geological surveys.

### Death Valley (USA)
- **Coordinates**: ~36.2°N, ~116.8°W
- **Why considered**: Alluvial fans, playas, salt flats, extreme diurnal temperature range. Used for rover testing and playa geomorphology.
- **Relevant properties**: Fan morphology, playa surfaces, thermal extremes, dust
- **Limitations**: Hot (not cold); gravity/atmosphere mismatch; Earth-specific weathering patterns.
- **Data availability**: Excellent — SRTM, ASTER GDEM, Landsat, Sentinel-2, USGS geological maps.

### Antarctica — McMurdo Dry Valleys
- **Coordinates**: ~77.5°S, ~162°E
- **Why considered**: One of Earth's driest and coldest environments. Permafrost processes resemble hypothesized Mars subsurface ice dynamics.
- **Relevant properties**: Cold aridity, permafrost, wind-sculpted terrain, endolithic biology
- **Limitations**: No SRTM coverage; must use REMA (Reference Elevation Model of Antarctica). Ice-sheet interior geology is not analogous to Moon or Mars surfaces.
- **Data availability**: REMA (~2–8 m); Landsat (seasonal); limited geological coverage.

### Lonar Crater (India)
- **Coordinates**: ~19.98°N, ~76.51°E
- **Crater diameter**: ~1.8 km
- **Why considered**: One of few terrestrial impact craters formed in basaltic target rock (Deccan Traps). Hydrothermal alteration studied (Hagerty & Newsom 2003, *MAPS* [verify]).
- **Relevant properties**: Impact in basalt (analogous to lunar mare and Mars basalts), crater degradation, saline-alkaline lake
- **Analog hypothesis**: Screening candidate for studying impact morphology in basaltic terrain comparable to lunar mare impacts.
- **Limitations**: Small crater; monsoon climate with seasonal flooding; heavy visitation; vegetation around crater rim.
- **Data availability**: SRTM, ASTER GDEM, Copernicus DEM cover the area. Landsat/Sentinel-2 available. GSI geological maps require Bhukosh access.

### Ladakh (India)
- **Coordinates**: ~32–36°N, ~75–80°E
- **Elevation**: 3,000–5,500+ m
- **Why considered**: High-altitude cold desert with periglacial features, saline lakes, Indus suture ophiolites, Puga geothermal area.
- **Analog hypothesis**: Screening candidate for cold-desert terrain comparison and periglacial landform studies. Hydrothermal/serpentinized rock settings may have astrobiological relevance.
- **Limitations**: Atmospheric pressure still ~half of sea level at 5,000 m (Mars is ~1% of sea level). Mineralogic Mars/Moon similarity is **unproven** — this is a hypothesis, not a finding.
- **Data availability**: SRTM, Copernicus DEM, ASTER GDEM. Landsat/Sentinel-2. ISRO Bhuvan and GSI Bhukosh for Indian-specific layers (registration may be required).

### Rann of Kutch (India)
- **Coordinates**: ~23.5–24.5°N, ~68.5–71.5°E
- **Why considered**: Hypothesis — vast seasonal salt flat may serve as a trafficability analog for playa/evaporite surfaces on Mars.
- **Analog hypothesis**: Weakest candidate in the list. No established analog literature for lunar or Martian comparison [verify].
- **Limitations**: Extremely flat (unlike rugged lunar polar terrain); monsoon flooding annually; salt chemistry differs from Mars sulfates; not morphologically similar to any known Artemis landing site.
- **Data availability**: SRTM, Copernicus DEM; Landsat/Sentinel-2; subject to seasonal variation in imagery.

> [!WARNING]
> **Honest assessment of Indian sites (Feature 5)**: Lonar is justifiable with published literature. Ladakh is a defensible exploratory hypothesis. Rann of Kutch is, on current evidence, a weak candidate. Present these as screening hypotheses, not discoveries. If the data gives a weak result, report it transparently. A transparent negative is more credible than a forced positive.

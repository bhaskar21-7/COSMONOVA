# Challenge Understanding

**COSMONOVA — NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026**  
*"Identify Earth Locations that Analog the Permanent Moon Base Locations and Mars"*

> [!NOTE]
> This document records our interpretation of the challenge. All interpretations should be verified against the official challenge description on [spaceappschallenge.org](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/) when it is published.

---

## Official Challenge Statement (Paraphrased)

Landing and base site selection for the Moon and Mars depends on topography, geology, environment, and resources. Earth analog sites — such as the Atacama Desert, Haughton Crater, and Death Valley — allow teams to test equipment and validate methods. However, these analog sites are **"not entirely well characterized."**

The challenge asks participants to:
1. Use open Earth, Moon, and Mars data
2. Identify and characterize terrestrial analogs
3. Draw on deserts, polar regions, volcanic terrain, arid environments, caves, and other settings
4. Present findings as a map, visualization, or other tool

## Critical Reading

### What "Characterize" Means
The challenge is not just about *finding* analogs — it is about *characterizing* them. Judges may value a rigorous characterization of a few sites over a broad, shallow map of many.

### Caves Are Explicitly Named
Lava tubes are relevant for both lunar and Martian habitat concepts. The challenge mentions caves as a category. Consider including at least one lava-tube region (e.g., Lanzarote, Lava Beds National Monument, Hawaiian tubes, Icelandic tubes).

**Reference**: Léveillé, R. J. & Datta, S. (2010). Lava tubes and basaltic caves as astrobiological targets on Earth and Mars. *Planetary and Space Science* 58.

### "New" Analogs Add Value
Re-ranking Atacama and Death Valley is legitimate but not differentiating. The value lies in what is **under-characterized** — sites with limited prior analog literature but plausible scientific rationale.

### Open Data Requirement
Everything used must be publicly accessible. This forces reproducibility and is a strength, not a limitation.

## Expected Judging Criteria

Based on prior NASA Space Apps rubrics (to be verified for 2026):

| Criterion | What It Means for Us |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Impact** | Does the tool help real researchers or mission planners? |
| **Creativity** | Is the approach novel — e.g., failure analysis, uncertainty quantification? |
| **Validity** | Are claims scientifically supported? Over-claiming loses here. |
| **Relevance** | Does it address the challenge question directly? |
| **Presentation** | Is the output clear, visual, and accessible? |

> [!WARNING]
> "Validity" is where most teams lose. A tool that says "this site is 87% similar to the Moon" without stating assumptions, uncertainty, or limitations will be viewed skeptically by scientific judges.

## Our Interpretation: Key Decisions

1. **Focus on characterization depth over breadth** — a few sites done well beats many sites done poorly.
2. **Mission-specific comparison** — analogs are always for a *purpose*, not in general.
3. **Report failures explicitly** — what a site *cannot* match is as informative as what it can.
4. **Quantify uncertainty** — confidence intervals and coverage fractions, not false precision.
5. **Include under-studied sites** — Indian candidates (Lonar, Ladakh) as screening hypotheses, with appropriate evidence caveats.

## Scope Boundaries

### In Scope
- Terrain, thermal, and geological property comparison
- Lunar south-polar target environment (Artemis regions)
- Mars target environment (Jezero/Gale as reference)
- 4–5 Earth candidate sites
- Interactive map and vector comparison output

### Out of Scope (for the hackathon)
- Atmospheric or radiation analogs (not reproducible on Earth)
- Gravity analogs (requires physical apparatus, not data)
- Operational/logistics analogs (separate research domain)
- Global automated scanning of all Earth surfaces
- Deep learning for "analogness" (no labeled training data exists)
